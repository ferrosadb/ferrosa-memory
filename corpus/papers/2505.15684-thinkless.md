# ThinkLess: A Training-Free Inference-Efficient Method for Reducing Reasoning Redundancy

**Authors:** Gengyang Li, Yifeng Gao, Yuming Li, Yunfang Wu
**arXiv:** [2505.15684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15684)
**Published:** May 2025

---

## Summary

Chain-of-Thought prompting improves reasoning quality but generates many redundant tokens before reaching the answer — inflating latency, KV-cache memory, and cost, and sometimes truncating final answers under context limits. ThinkLess discovers through attention analysis that answer tokens attend primarily to the *reasoning terminator token* (e.g., `</think>`), not to the earlier reasoning steps. Under causal masking, information migrates to the terminator token during generation. Inserting the terminator earlier therefore skips redundant reasoning while preserving the information transfer, with no fine-tuning required.

---

## Key Findings

- **Answer tokens attend to terminators, not reasoning steps.** Attention weight analysis shows that the final answer generation depends heavily on a single terminator token and minimally on the hundreds of prior reasoning tokens.
- **Early terminator insertion works.** Inserting `</think>` before the full reasoning chain completes produces comparable accuracy to full-length CoT on tested benchmarks.
- **No fine-tuning required.** The effect is a property of causal masking and information migration — it generalizes without any model modification.
- **Lightweight post-regulation:** Early termination can produce malformed outputs (incomplete sentences, missing structure). A lightweight mechanism uses the model's natural instruction-following ability to regularize the output format without additional training.
- **Substantial compute savings:** Dramatic reduction in decoding time and KV-cache memory when the reasoning chain is shortened.

---

## Key Limitations

- Effect quality varies by model — models with weaker instruction-following may produce poorly formatted outputs after early termination.
- Not suitable for tasks where the reasoning process itself is the required output (e.g., showing work on a math problem).

---

## Ferrosa Memory MCP Relevance

ThinkLess's core finding — that the terminator token carries disproportionate informational weight relative to the full reasoning trace — has a direct architectural implication for how memory content should be stored and indexed:

- **Store the terminator-equivalent, not the full reasoning trace.** For sub-call results stored in `memo_cache`, the most valuable content to store is the *answer* plus the *conclusion summary* — not the full chain-of-thought that produced them. The NL capsule format (from NL-Compress) is the practical implementation of this: it captures the terminator-equivalent information in a compact, retrievable form.
- **`fold_summary` as information-maximizing compression.** When `complete_fold` writes a fold summary, it should target the terminator-equivalent information: the outcome, the key findings, and the state changes — not a truncated version of the trajectory. The ThinkLess result validates that this is not lossy in the sense that matters — downstream answer quality depends on this summary, not on the reasoning steps.
- **Index on summary column, not raw content.** The HNSW index on `fold_embedding` should be computed from the embedding of the `fold_summary` (the terminator-equivalent), not from the raw trajectory. Retrieval based on the summary embedding is more accurate because the summary contains the semantically dense information that downstream queries actually need.
- **ThinkLess-style early stopping for sub-call generation.** For sub-calls within the RLM loop that are making progress on routine tasks, the MCP server can suggest early termination by including a `terminator_hint` in the tool response — a structured cue that the LLM has enough information to answer without further reasoning. This is an optional v2 feature that requires prompt engineering work.
